The time, written
the medieval way.

Ciscrux is a free screen saver for your Mac. It shows the year, the date, the time and the seconds in Cistercian numerals, each as a single figure, and draws every new figure stroke by stroke.

Download for Mac

Free · macOS 11 or later · Apple silicon and Intel

How to read it

Four corners, one number.

Each figure is a vertical line with up to four marks around it. The mark in the upper right is the ones, the upper left the tens, the lower right the hundreds and the lower left the thousands. So a single figure writes any number from 1 to 9999.

Ciscrux writes the date as month and day (1113 is November 13) and the time as hour and minute on a 24-hour clock (1452 is 14:52).

The seconds figure

The weekday and the moon, in the spare corners.

Seconds only need the top two corners, so Ciscrux uses the bottom two as well: the lower right holds the day of the week and the lower left the moon phase. With Show Hint on, a small moon on the left shows the phase, and the symbol on the right is the planet of the day.

Install

Three steps, then pick it in Wallpaper.

Ciscrux is signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple.

  1. Download

    Download Ciscrux.zip and open it.

  2. Install

    Double-click Ciscrux.saver. macOS asks whether to install the screen saver; click Install.

  3. Choose Ciscrux

    Open the screen saver settings and pick Ciscrux:

    System Settings › Wallpaper › Screen Saver…

Can't find the screen saver settings? On recent macOS they aren't in the sidebar on their own: open Wallpaper and click Screen Saver… near the bottom.

On older versions, look for Screen Saver in System Settings or System Preferences.

Click Options… next to Ciscrux to change what it shows.

Or with Homebrew:
brew install --cask vulcanshen/tap/ciscrux

Nothing leaves your Mac.

Ciscrux has no network code at all. It collects no data and only keeps its options on your Mac. Read the Privacy Policy.

Why “Ciscrux”?

Cistercian, plus a cross.

cis- from Cistercian, plus crux, Latin for “cross”, as in the crux of the matter. Crux is also the constellation of the Southern Cross.
