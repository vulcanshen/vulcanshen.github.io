Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 6, 2026

HelpKnock has no server and no account: what you enter stays on your phone and goes only to phones nearby over Bluetooth, and to rescuers on the same Wi-Fi, never to the developer.

What HelpKnock collects

HelpKnock has no server and no account. The developer never receives anything you enter. Apart from the optional support for the developer described below, the app does not connect to the internet and uses no analytics or third-party services.

Where your data stays

Your personal, medical and rescuer details, and the situation report you fill in while asking for help, are stored in this phone's keychain, on this device only. None of it is included in iCloud or computer backups. Personal details stay until you clear or change them in HelpKnock's Settings; the situation report is erased when you leave the “I need help” role.

The list of nearby phones and what you load from them is kept in memory only and is gone when the app is closed. Messages are different: they stay on this phone as a record until you delete them.

Erasing all data in HelpKnock's Settings removes all of the above at once, including what deleting the app leaves behind in the keychain.

What is sent over Bluetooth

In either role, your phone broadcasts a short beacon to every phone nearby running HelpKnock. It contains a random node ID, your role, whether you have reported yourself safe, your battery level, minutes since you last touched the phone, and the headcount and hazards you set. In the “I need help” role, it also contains how long the phone has been still. If you set the language you speak in your details, that is included too. While position sharing is on, the beacon also carries the phone's last known position with its accuracy and age; sharing is on by default when you take a role, and you can switch it off on HelpKnock's home screen.

While you are in the “I need help” role, a nearby phone in the rescuer role can connect and read the fields you turned on in “What others can see”, plus your situation, needs and passive signals such as battery trend and time since you last touched the phone. Only those fields are sent. Anyone nearby who chose the rescuer role can read them; the app cannot verify who they are.

In the rescuer role, the unit, callsign and capabilities you entered can be read by every phone nearby running HelpKnock, including people asking for help, so they can see who you say you are. Your marks and messages are shared with other rescuers in range.

Messages are shared too. What you write in the “I need help” role reaches every rescuer nearby, and rescuers pass it on to each other, so each of their phones keeps a copy. What rescuers write to you is passed on the same way.

Reports are shared too. A report, with the name of the person who made it, is passed on to rescuers nearby; a report about a rescuer also reaches people asking for help. Blocking someone only changes your own phone: their messages are hidden, and while you are in the “I need help” role they can no longer read your details.

What is shared over Wi-Fi

In either role, while the app is on screen and for a short while after you leave it, HelpKnock looks for rescuers on the same Wi-Fi network or phone hotspot and connects to them directly, alongside Bluetooth. People asking for help connect only to rescuers, not to each other. Nothing goes through the internet or a server. Other devices on that network can see that a phone running HelpKnock is there, with its role and random node ID.

Over this connection rescuers share the same things they pass on to each other over Bluetooth: their callsigns, marks, messages, reports and teams, and the beacons they have picked up from people asking for help, position included. The details a rescuer reads from a person asking for help are not passed on. Anyone on the same network who chose the rescuer role receives all of this; the app cannot verify who they are.

In the “I need help” role, rescuers on the same Wi-Fi get what they would get over Bluetooth: your beacon, position included, your messages, and the fields you turned on in “What others can see”, plus your situation, needs and passive signals. Anyone on the same network who chose the rescuer role can read them; the app cannot verify who they are. With the screen locked, only Bluetooth is used.

Not encrypted yet

Bluetooth and Wi-Fi transfers are not encrypted in this version. Someone within radio range or on the same network with the right equipment could read what is sent. Share only what you would say out loud to a stranger who came to help.

Node ID

The node ID is random and changes every time you leave a role, so phones cannot be tracked from one session to the next. It is not linked to your Apple Account, phone number or anything else.

Permissions

The app asks for four permissions. Bluetooth is used to broadcast and to find nearby phones, including while the app is in the background, so a trapped person can still be found with the screen off. Location is asked for on the role screen or when you take a role, and is used only to put your last known position in the broadcast; the app does not track you in the background. Motion & Fitness is asked for on the role screen or in the “I need help” role, and is used only to read how long the phone has been still, from the motion record the phone already keeps; the app does not switch on any sensor. Local Network is asked for on the role screen or when you take a role, and is used only to find and connect to rescuers on the same Wi-Fi; without it, only Bluetooth is used.

Third parties

  • Apple in-app purchase: supporting the developer in HelpKnock's Settings is optional. When you open HelpKnock's Settings, the app asks the App Store for the prices; if you choose an amount, Apple handles the payment. The developer is not told who you are, and HelpKnock keeps nothing about the purchase.

If this policy changes, this page will be updated along with the date above.
